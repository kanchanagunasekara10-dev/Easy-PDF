Turning photos, scans, and screenshots into a document

Almost nobody owns a flatbed scanner any more, and almost everybody carries a camera good enough to replace one. That shift is why this conversion is so common: you photograph a stack of certificates, a signed form, a page of handwritten notes, or a month of receipts, and end up with a folder full of loose picture files. What you actually need is one document, in the right order, that can be emailed, printed, or uploaded to a portal that accepts PDF and nothing else.

This tool handles JPG and JPEG files, which is what phone cameras produce, and PNG files, which is what screenshots and most design exports produce. You can mix both in the same document, so a batch made up of two camera photos and three screenshots converts perfectly well. If you are working from screenshots of a chat or a web page, PNG usually gives you sharper text than a re-saved JPG would.

Order matters more than people expect. A three-page contract photographed out of sequence is a three-page problem for whoever receives it, so the reordering arrows are worth using before you convert rather than fixing afterwards in a PDF editor.

Choosing the right page size

The page size setting is the one option that most affects how your finished document looks, and the best choice depends entirely on what the PDF is for.

A4 and Letter

These produce standard printable pages, 210 by 297 millimetres for A4 and 8.5 by 11 inches for Letter, with each image scaled down to fit inside while keeping its original proportions. Choose one of these when the document will be printed, emailed to an office, or submitted to a government or university portal, since those systems usually expect conventional page dimensions. A4 is the standard almost everywhere outside North America, including Sri Lanka, so it is the default here. Images that do not match the page proportions will have white space above and below, or to the sides, which is normal and expected for printed documents.

Fit to image

This makes every page exactly the size of the image sitting on it, so there is no white space at all and no scaling. It is the right choice for screen reading, for scanned comics or artwork where borders would be distracting, and for screenshots where you want pixel dimensions preserved. The trade-off is that pages can end up different sizes from one another, which looks unusual when printed.

Margins

Margins add breathing room around each image. A small margin generally looks more polished for documents that will be printed, and it also protects your content from being clipped, since most desktop printers cannot print right to the edge of the paper. Choose no margin when you want the image to fill the page completely, which pairs naturally with the fit-to-image setting.

Frequently asked questions

How many images can I combine into one PDF?

There is no fixed limit. The practical ceiling is your device's available memory, since the whole document is assembled locally. Most computers handle several hundred photos comfortably, while phones do better with smaller batches of around fifty images at a time.

Does converting to PDF reduce my image quality?

No. Your original JPG data is embedded directly into the PDF without being re-encoded, so there is no additional compression or quality loss. The image inside the PDF is byte-for-byte the same picture you started with.

Can I convert PNG files as well as JPG?

Yes. The tool accepts JPG, JPEG, and PNG images, and you can mix formats in the same document. PNG transparency is flattened against the page background, which is standard behaviour for PDF.

What is the difference between A4 and fit-to-image page size?

A4 and Letter create standard printable pages and scale each image to fit inside, which is best when the PDF will be printed or submitted to an official portal. Fit-to-image makes each page exactly the size of the picture on it, which avoids white borders and suits screen viewing or comics and scanned artwork.

Can I change the order of images before converting?

Yes. Each thumbnail has left and right arrow buttons that move it earlier or later in the sequence, and an X button to remove it. The order shown on screen is exactly the page order in the finished PDF.

Is there a file size limit?

Individual files above 50MB are skipped to protect your browser from running out of memory. That is far above a typical photo, which is usually between 2MB and 8MB even from a high-end phone camera.

How do I convert a PDF back into images?

Use the PDF to JPG converter, which does the opposite job. It renders every page of a PDF as a separate JPG image and hands multi-page documents back to you as a ZIP archive.
