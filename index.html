Which converter do you need?

The two tools are mirror images of each other, so picking the right one comes down to what you are holding and what you need next.

If you have pictures and you need a document, you want the JPG to PDF converter. This is the direction most people need: you have photographed a stack of receipts, screenshotted a conversation, or scanned some paperwork with a phone camera, and now something official expects a single PDF rather than a folder of loose image files. The converter stacks your pictures into one document in whatever order you choose.

If you have a document and you need pictures, you want the PDF to JPG converter. This is the direction you need when a PDF will not preview properly in a chat app, when you want to post a certificate or a slide to social media, or when you need to crop one page and drop it into a presentation. Every page comes back as a separate image.

Both directions are lossless in the sense that matters: converting an image to PDF embeds your original file without re-compressing it, while converting a PDF to images lets you pick the resolution rather than accepting whatever a server decides for you.

Why convert in your browser instead of on a server?

Most online file converters work by uploading your document to a company's server, processing it there, and sending a result back to you. That model works, but it means a copy of your file, whether it is a passport scan, a signed contract, or a page of medical records, sits on hardware you do not control. Many services promise to delete uploads after an hour, and most of them do, but you are still trusting a promise.

EasyPDF takes a different approach. Both converters are built on JavaScript libraries that run inside the browser tab you already have open, using the same rendering engine that displays this page. When you pick a file, the browser reads it into memory on your own device, does the conversion locally, and hands you a download. No network request carries your file anywhere. You can confirm this yourself by opening your browser's developer tools, switching to the Network tab, and watching that nothing is uploaded during a conversion. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tools will keep working.

The practical benefits go beyond privacy. There is no upload wait and no download wait, so conversions finish as fast as your own device can process them, which for a handful of images is usually under a second. There are no queues during busy periods, no daily limits, and no file counter quietly pushing you toward a paid plan.

Frequently asked questions

Is EasyPDF really free?

Yes. There is no account to create, no trial period, and no limit on how many files you convert. Because conversions happen on your own device rather than on rented servers, there are no per-file costs to pass on to you.

Do my files get uploaded anywhere?

No. Your files are read into your browser's memory, converted locally, and offered back to you as a download. Nothing is transmitted to us or to any third party. Once you close or refresh the tab, the data is gone from memory entirely.

Which converter should I use?

If you have photos or screenshots and need a document, use the JPG to PDF converter. If you already have a PDF and need the pages as pictures, use the PDF to JPG converter. The two tools are exact opposites of each other.

Will there be a watermark on my files?

Never. Converted files contain only your own content, with no logos, stamps, or trial notices added.

Does this work on a phone or tablet?

Yes. Both tools work in any modern mobile browser, including Chrome on Android and Safari on iOS. On a phone you can pick images straight from your camera roll or take a new photo. Very large files may run slower on mobile because conversion uses your device's own memory.

Do I need to install anything?

No. There is no software to download, no browser extension, and no plugin. Open the page and start converting.
